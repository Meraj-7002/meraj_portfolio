01 · Deal Engine · PerfectlyOkay

Scraping jobs that survive a dead worker.

Collects offers from 26 affiliate networks, checks coupons, and runs price search across 6 quick-commerce platforms including Blinkit, Zepto and JioMart. Scraping runs on remote workers, so the queue has to cope with a worker disappearing mid-job.

  • Database migration. Moved 24 MySQL tables to MongoDB, rebuilt 24 of the 26 network integrations on it, and retired the old code. Each project gets physically separate collections, so client data stays isolated.
  • A paging bug. In testing, skip/limit paging lost 1 of 10 offers during a concurrent delete. Keyset paging fixed it.
  • Browser takeover. Live browser streaming lets a person take control of a remote session, and control actions are audited.
  • Coupon checks. The WooCommerce checker tries the Store API, then a proxy, then stealth Chrome.

02 · Deal Platform · PerfectlyOkay

An admin dashboard that stopped scanning whole collections.

The public deal storefront and admin console, kept in sync with the Deal Engine. My work here is query performance, withdrawal safety, search correctness and the sync between the two systems.

  • Proving a rewrite safe. After rewriting the dashboard queries I compared old and new responses value by value: 3,029 of 3,140 were bit-identical and the rest were within rounding.
  • Double spend. Closed a concurrent double-spend hole with atomic withdrawal claims.
  • Wrong search results. Admin store search gave wrong results for 502 of 1,151 filter combinations. All were correct after the fix.
  • AI guardrails. Four checks run after the Deal Assistant generates a reply, catching false verification claims, cashback claims, percentages and code-shaped tokens. Accuracy checks went from 22/43 to 43/43 on 19 prompts.
  • Saying no. I rejected optimizations that didn't hold up when measured, including a covering index that made a query worse.

03 · BrandSecurer

Finding the affiliate hiding behind a redirect chain.

A brand-protection product. It monitors 8 search engines across 42 selectable countries, follows ads through their redirects and identifies the affiliate bidding on a brand's name.

  • False blocks. A CAPTCHA check marked 12 of 22 healthy pages as blocked, cutting one run from 870 candidates to 237. I found and fixed it.
  • Dropped data. A certificate stream dropped over 5M certificates on the shared web thread. I moved it to its own process.
  • Billing. Paddle webhooks are signature-verified and deduplicated, with failed events replayed. Reconciliation runs every 15 minutes, and I moved the double-charge guard from client to server.
  • Security. TOTP 2FA with hashed recovery codes, SSRF checks on every HTTP redirect hop, and sliding-window rate limiting on login and other routes.

04 · Backupper

Restores you can preview before they touch the server.

Backup, restore and monitoring for remote Linux servers over SSH, covering files, MongoDB and MySQL. A restore is the riskiest moment for a server, so it runs in checked stages.

  • My part in restore. I started this flow and a colleague later extended it. The database diff compares collection and table names, not rows.
  • Resource limits. Backups run under systemd cgroup CPU and memory limits, read back from inside the scope. If they can't be applied, the job fails.
  • Large exports. Streaming NDJSON avoided about 15 GB of RAM on a 9 GB export in testing.
  • Monitoring. Spike detection against a 7-day median baseline, least-squares disk forecasting up to 30 days ahead, and a missed-backup sweep every 15 minutes.
